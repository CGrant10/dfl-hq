Trade Analyzer — three directions

The problem is the order. You open this page because somebody offered you a trade, and the answer — ACCEPT / PASS / NEGOTIATE — is the last thing on it, under two scrolling checkbox lists of thirty players each. At 390px you cannot see the verdict and the deal at once, so every change to the package is scroll down, read, scroll back.

Three secondary things follow: the recommendation is a small chip beside a bigger word, so the loudest element is not the advice; balance is a bare percentage when verdictFor() already knows 88+ is even and under 55 is lopsided; and two side-by-side lists is a desktop pattern.

No number here is invented. Everything shown is already produced by evaluateTrade() — fairness, value out per side, weekly lineup delta per side, and the four ranked reasons with their tones. Drawn in the live palette (tokens.css + style.css), so the colour is honest.

What changes:

  • The verdict is first and the call is the biggest word on it.
  • The beam tilts by the fairness number, so the shape of the deal reads before any text does. The meter behind it draws verdictFor()'s four bands.
  • Two checkbox columns become chips you add and remove, with one roster at a time in a sheet — so a package is editable without leaving the verdict.
Cost: the most work of the three. New selection flow, and the sheet has to handle multi-team deals (the desk already supports 3+ parties).

What changes:

  • The trade becomes a document — two columns totalled like an invoice, so "who gave up more" is arithmetic you can see rather than a percentage you have to trust.
  • The call is a stamp across the middle. Unmissable.
  • It is shareable. This is the same shape as the Sportsbook entry card that already ships, so it can go to the group chat as an image — which is where trade arguments actually happen.
  • Selection moves behind "Edit deal", so the reading state and the building state are separate screens.
Cost: middling. The ticket itself is layout, but a share card means a second canvas renderer, and hiding the builder is the biggest behaviour change of the three.

Team Analyzer — three directions

The page already admits the problem. A comment in js/pages/analyzer.js reads: "The report ran to eighteen screens on a phone." Density took a third off and two sections were folded — but the shape underneath is still one continuous scroll of seven sections that all look equally important.

Four things follow. There is no answer at the top: the lead prints three grade cards, four KPIs and a top asset — eight numbers at the same size, none of them a headline. Everything is a table: the roster table has nine columns, which at 390px becomes nine label/value pairs per player, so fifteen players is 135 of them. Season Outlook is a separate panel above the report, so the simulated odds sit visually apart from the grades that produced them. And the five unit grades are the model's core idea — starter grade is their equal average — yet they are a table column, never a picture, so a roster's shape is never drawn.

No number here is invented. Grades, percentiles, league ranks, weekly and expected points, position ranks, trade values, trends, the strength/need strings and the playoff/title/Chip Eater odds all already come out of team-analyzer.js and season-outlook.js.

What changes:

  • One headline grade at 60px answers "is my team good" before anything is read. The other two grades demote to a strip.
  • The roster's shape gets drawn. Five bars, filled by the percentiles the model already computes — elite RB, thin flex, visible in one glance. This is the single biggest gain of any direction here.
  • The odds move up beside the grades that produced them; Season Outlook stops being a floating panel.
  • Nine table columns become four facts and a bar; the rest goes behind a tap.
  • Tabs cap the page at one screen of chrome instead of seven sections of scroll.
Cost: middling. Tabs mean the deep-link (?team=) should carry a tab too, and the folded-details pattern gets replaced.

What changes:

  • One scroll is kept — a report you read start to finish is a real use, and tabs hide what you did not think to look for.
  • What changes is rhythm: a full-bleed masthead with the projected record at 52px, then bands that alternate surface and each open with an eyebrow and a rule out to a count. Scrolling gets landmarks.
  • The lead's eight equal numbers become one sentence that names the finding, with the figures bolded inside it. outlookSentence() already writes this kind of prose.
  • The nine-column table becomes a two-column card grid; depth is the same card at 72% opacity, which is a quieter divider than a full-width caps row.
Cost: the least. No new navigation, no deep-link changes — this is a restyle plus turning one table into a grid. Also the safest for anyone who reads the report top to bottom today.
